# Brewmaster's Bazaar — Omar the Wanderer
## Traveling Merchant Dialogue Lines (215 Lines)
*Voice: Moroccan souk vendor × wise old bartender × Marco Polo*
*Warm. Sly. Occasionally poetic. Always knows more than he's saying.*

---

## CHARACTER NOTES

**Name:** Omar the Wanderer  
**Full Title (rarely used):** Omar ibn Khalid al-Qayrawan, Merchant of Seven Roads  
**Voice archetype:** Imagine a man who has haggled in every language, tasted every fermented thing on earth, and still finds the world endlessly interesting. He is never in a hurry. He is never surprised. He has seen your type before — and he is genuinely glad you're here.  
**Signature phrases:** "Ah..." (pause) | "You know what I find interesting?" | "I have a story about that." | "...but perhaps that is not your concern." | "Between you and me—"  
**Never:** Rude. Genuinely offensive. Hurried. Desperate.

[story_sakura]: "Cherry blossoms. They bloom for ten days. Ten days, and then they fall, and the whole country mourns something they knew was temporary the whole time. [story_koji]: "Koji is not an ingredient. Koji is a colleague. A very old, very reliable colleague who will do extraordinary things to any substrate you give it. ## Southeast Asian Ingredients

## Latin American Ingredients

[story_cacao]: "The Aztecs used cacao as currency. I think about that constantly when I trade it — I am selling money. [story_vanilla]: "A twelve-year-old boy on the island of Réunion figured out how to hand-pollinate vanilla with a stick in 1841. One child, one discovery, changed the global spice trade forever. [story_worm_salt]: "The mezcal worm in cheap tequila bottles — marketing invention. A gimmick. The real tradition is the worm salt: dried agave larvae, ground with salt and chili. [story_hibiscus]: "I carry Jamaica — hibiscus — under four different names for four different customers: *jamaica* for the Mexicans, *bissap* for the West Africans, *karkadé* for the Egyptians, *roselle* for the English traders. [story_piloncillo]: "Real piloncillo is not the pale tan cones you see everywhere. Real piloncillo is dark — almost black — pressed from cane that has not been bleached or stripped of its molasses. [story_rooibos]: "A Russian immigrant named Benjamin Ginsberg discovered rooibos in 1904 when he saw the Khoisan people harvesting the red needles from the Cederberg mountains.
